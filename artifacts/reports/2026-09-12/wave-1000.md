### #25 fixed — trust-store docs contradicted the audit (docs truth-up)
README.md:23 and :99, auckland-vpn:13, and the AGENTS.md design bullet claimed
trust in "the system's / Mac's built-in Certificate Authorities". The audit
(docs/consults/openconnect-audit.md §160, §168, §172, all CONFIRMED) found the

(Homebrew `ca-certificates`), not Keychain trust; local evidence agrees —
`otool -L /opt/homebrew/bin/openconnect` shows `libgnutls.30.dylib` and no
Security.framework trust path. All four sites now state the GnuTLS/Mozilla
reality per the audit's §172 recommended wording, plus a troubleshooting note
that a Keychain-added root is *not* automatically trusted. Residual grep is
clean; the remaining "built-in CAs" text lives only where historical reports
quote the old claim as their own finding (audit §168, docs/reports/
2026-09-10-quick-scan.md:67).

### #55 filed — stat/mktemp PATH-hardening drift (new quick-scan finding)
Scanning the blind spot carried over from the 09-10/09-11/0930 reports
(macOS/portability sensitivity of `stat -f`/`mktemp`): the script hardens
security-sensitive reads with `/usr/bin/stat` / `/usr/bin/mktemp`
(auckland-vpn:539, :541, :576, :626) but leaves 7 unqualified call sites
(:236, :238, :258, :887, :1004, :1331, :1403). With GNU coreutils shadowing
PATH, :1403's `stat -f '%z' … || echo 0` silently disables monitor-log
rotation and :234/:236 false-refuse startup. Latent on this machine
(`command -v stat` → /usr/bin/stat). Ticketed as #55 with implement-as-todo;
not fixed this wave to keep the commit docs-only and reviewable.
